## What it is

**DOCUMENTED:** Stroud explicitly says the book is neither a general introduction to all solid-modeling approaches nor a description/review of a particular commercial system. It develops an idealized B-rep environment, elementary operations, stepwise modeling algorithms, information handling, graphics, exchange, free-form geometry and applications. The algorithms are examples, not a complete kernel. Appendix A principally reflects BUILD and the GPM volume module, with extensions. [FM preface pp.vii–viii](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bfm:978-1-84628-616-2/1); [BM p.609](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**INFERRED:** it is a much better source for concrete data contracts and historical failure modes than product brochures, but **not** evidence of unpublished modern Parasolid/ACIS implementations. The most valuable surprise is that the public back matter contains substantial implementation detail, not just an index. [BM](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

## How it works

### Topology and geometry are distinct, with several valid representations

**DOCUMENTED:** Appendix A gives pointer-based variants rather than mandating one layout. A vertex links to a point; an edge links to its curve and endpoints; a face links to its surface, orientation and boundary loops; a loop distinguishes perimeter from hole. Faces can have multiple loops, and a vertex-only loop is a recognized degenerate representation. Separate shells avoid recovering closure by repeated traversal. Face groups may collect entities sharing a surface or construction operation; information/name links are separate from geometry. [BM §§A.1.1–A.1.10, pp.609–619](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**DOCUMENTED:** edge-layout alternatives include direct winged-edge pointers (right/left clockwise/counterclockwise plus right/left loop), loop-edge links, vertex-edge links, and nonmanifold star/wedge variants. A loop-edge link carries edge, loop, next and previous; the star form additionally cycles incident links around an edge. The text warns that a star alone can leave body connectivity ambiguous; duplicated edges or wedges distinguish the local sectors. Do not confuse geometric coincidence, a shared support curve, and topological identity. [BM §§A.1.2–A.1.6, pp.611–616](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

### Intersection is a typed geometric service, not a Boolean-valued helper

**DOCUMENTED:** Appendix D's intersection interface dispatches by point/curve/surface type and then specific analytic pair, including plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus and spline patch. Suggested result kinds include same-orientation coincidence, reverse-orientation coincidence, point, curve, self-intersecting curve and surface, or sets of these. Partial coincidence may return a separate geometric entity representing the common part. Domain-limiting boxes/spheres belong in the input contract when approximating infinite intersections. [BM §D.1, pp.673–676](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**DOCUMENTED:** the benefit of specialized analytic dispatch is not merely speed: special cases can be unstable under generic numerical intersection but recognizable analytically. Plane/cylinder classification in `pla_cyl_int` is explicit: if the plane normal is perpendicular to the cylinder axis, compare axis-to-plane distance with radius to obtain zero, one or two generators; otherwise the intersection is a circle or ellipse. The center is the plane/axis intersection, and the transverse ellipse direction follows the normal/axis cross product. [BM §D.1.1, pp.680–681, Fig.D.5](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**INFERRED implementable restatement, independently derived:** for unit cylinder axis a, unit plane normal n, radius r and q=|n·a|, the nonparallel-to-axis cut has ellipse semiaxes r and r/q; q=1 is the circular case, q=0 requires the generator classification. This does **not** solve the clipped face Boolean: both faces' trim loops must still be intersected and classified. Near q=0, the construction becomes poorly conditioned and requires a domain-aware/error-bounded branch, not a naked epsilon test. The analytic case split is supported by [BM pp.680–681](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1); the explicit normalized equation/error policy here is our derivation.

**DOCUMENTED:** for general cylinder/cylinder intersection, Stroud describes finding an approximate seed, alternately projecting between the cylinders to relax it onto both, then marching nearby seeds and repeating. He explicitly warns that near tangency this can jump onto the wrong intersection branch; Fig.D.11 illustrates the topology change. General spline/spline and torus/spline intersection are outside the appendix's scope. [BM §§D.1.2,D.1.5–D.1.6, pp.684–690](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

### Classification and clipping must retain topology context

**DOCUMENTED:** point-in-body has an explicit on-boundary check; grazing events do not count as true crossings. An oriented alternative uses the first true crossing: entering means the starting point is outside; leaving means inside. A box-outside shortcut is invalid for complemented/negative objects. [BM §E.3, pp.709–711, Figs.E.1–E.3](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**DOCUMENTED:** curve/face intersection clips against **every** boundary edge, including hole loops, sorts events by curve parameter and treats coincidences specially. On curved periodic faces, a test curve can wrap around a cylinder without meeting any real boundary edge; Fig.E.6 warns that the usual crossing argument then has no event to inspect. The source suggests artificial edges or a better test curve, and notes the vertex-only minimal face as another special case. [BM §§E.4–E.6, pp.711–714](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**DOCUMENTED:** local edge convexity uses s=(n_left×n_right)·t_edge under the book's orientation convention; s>0 means convex, s<0 concave and zero smooth. But an edge may change classification along its length: a cylinder/cylinder elliptical edge can be smooth at both endpoints and convex in the middle. One sample or the endpoints do not classify the entire edge. The book's vector-area loop orientation method is likewise explicitly limited, not a general periodic-surface classifier. [BM §§E.8–E.9, pp.715–716, Fig.E.9](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

### Euler operators preserve count relations, not geometric correctness

**DOCUMENTED:** the stated Euler–Poincaré relation is v−e+f−h=2(b−g), with h inner loops, b shells/multiplicity and g genus. A minimal set of five operators and inverses spans the allowed count changes, but practical operators add useful shortcuts. A count delta such as (+1 vertex,+1 edge) can mean adding a spur, splitting an edge or splitting a vertex. Therefore count changes do not uniquely specify topology edits. [BM Appendix F, pp.719–722](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**DOCUMENTED:** edge splitting (§F.2) needs the edge, an orientation/side choice and usually the new vertex position; it updates both edge uses and may need to adjust curve parameterization. Low-level operators can temporarily produce geometrically incorrect objects while a larger operation completes. Orientation decisions are often best supplied by the caller that knows the intended construction rather than guessed from transient geometry. [BM pp.722–728](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

### The Boolean implementation notes are candid, not a success proof

**DOCUMENTED:** Appendix G.1 reports that the GPM Boolean implementation was inefficient in FORTRAN IV, lacked convenient edge/vertex lists, and that replacement joining code was never completed. A tolerance failure can create an intersection curve that appears to enter a face but has no detectable exit because of geometric inconsistency. BUILD's comparison used the other face's surface while clipping, making edge/surface comparisons more stable than GPM's edge/curve comparisons. [BM pp.751–752, Fig.G.1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**DOCUMENTED:** joining whole intersection rings breaks when rings share edges. Extra matching tests only partly repaired the original code; a second design joined **edge pairs**, with operation/orientation case diagrams in Figs.G.4–G.7. That second design was only partially implemented, and the author explicitly says there are no comparative results. This is an excellent caution against treating diagram coverage as a demonstrated robust algorithm. [BM pp.753–757](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

## Robustness and guarantees

- **DOCUMENTED:** the text is an implementation reference using reals, integers, pointers, tolerances and geometric constructions; the passages read do not prescribe f64, exact rational arithmetic or a certified error model. Appendix D calls itself sketchy; generic numerical patch intersection is delegated to specialist texts. [BM pp.609,673,690](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).
- **INFERRED:** Euler identities are useful necessary consistency checks, not proof of manifoldness, valid embedding, absence of self-intersection, correct trim orientation or Boolean semantics. The text's explicit discussion of temporary geometric invalidity supports this distinction. [BM pp.719–722](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).
- **INFERRED, arithmetic caution:** do not transcribe the geometric formulas without dimensional/algebraic verification. For example, the printed line/line construction on p.677 uses a factor based on l/((P_q−P_2)·d_2), where l is drawn as a length and d_2 is normalized; as written that factor is dimensionless before addition to a position, suggesting a missing length factor or unstated convention. This is a reading-based concern, not a tested erratum. It reinforces using the source for case structure and re-deriving numerical formulas. [BM p.677](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

## Parallelism and performance

**DOCUMENTED:** no reproducible CPU/GPU measurements were found in the sections read. The FORTRAN IV observation is qualitative; the unfinished ring-join replacement has no benchmark. Pointer rewrites, entity marker bits and sequential traversals dominate the presented implementation idiom. Multi-user modeling appears in the TOC but was not read and must not be misrepresented as parallel geometry evidence. [BM §§E.1,G.1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1); [FM TOC](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bfm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

**INFERRED:** independent surface-pair dispatch and geometric evaluation can use fork-join; mutation-heavy Euler sequences cannot simply run concurrently on shared topology. Batch independent edits, resolve conflicts, allocate stable IDs and reconstruct affected incidence arrays. GPU work is most plausible for type-bucketed analytic evaluations, not irregular pointer traversal or adaptive marching. [BM Appendices A,D,F](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

## Known failures, limitations, war stories

**DOCUMENTED, primary firsthand implementation notes:** missing intersection exits from tolerance drift (Fig.G.1); shared-edge intersection rings defeating ring-wise joins (Figs.G.2–G.3); marching jumping branches near cylinder tangency (Fig.D.11); a circular point-in-face ray never crossing a cylinder's boundaries (Fig.E.6); edge endpoints being insufficient to determine whole-edge convexity (Fig.E.9); full-circle sweep accumulation breaking exact face gluing, and swept faces self-intersecting next to smaller adjacent faces (G.2, Fig.G.8). There is no linked public issue tracker; these book passages are the primary failure record. [BM](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

## Relevance for wonky

**INFERRED, immediate priorities:**

1. **SSI contract before solver expansion:** return an explicit tagged set of empty/point/curve/coincident-region results, with orientation, analytic source IDs, trim domain and uncertainty. Preserve both source surfaces when clipping an SSI curve; do not reduce everything prematurely to unrelated 3D polylines. This directly addresses the plane/cylinder union and analytic recovery path. [BM §§D.1,G.1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).
2. **Immutable topology edits:** encode an Euler-style edit as a checked change set (new entities, removed entities, changed edge uses/loops, expected count deltas and provenance), then construct a new topology snapshot. Keep typed operations such as split-edge versus split-vertex distinct even when their Euler deltas match. U32 IDs and sidecar flags fit Bend; pointers, mutation and global marker bits do not. [BM Appendices A,E.1,F](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).
3. **Numeric specialization:** keep analytic pair cases and explicit tangent/coincident branches. F32x2 can evaluate scaled constructions, but sign uncertainty near parallelism/tangency should trigger U32-limb predicate evaluation or an explicit unresolved result. Extra mantissa bits alone do not cure ill-conditioned construction or a missed SSI branch. No dependency on an f64 ABI is imposed by the prose, but no F32 safety proof is supplied either. [BM §D.1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).
4. **Regression corpus:** turn G.1/G.2/G.3, D.11, E.6 and E.9 into tiny hand-authored fixtures. Compare classification under input order reversal, scale changes and topology-preserving refinements; verify both interval/trim consistency and shell adjacency, not just v−e+f. The same diagnostics benefit fillet edge selection, STEP pcurves and FDM mesh validation. [BM cited figures](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).
5. **LLM/introspection ergonomics:** expose edit intent, domain-limited SSI results and classifier reasons, not opaque success booleans or an Euler count alone. This is a modern adaptation, not a facility claimed by the 2006 book. [BM suggested geometry-result categories and model information fields](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-1-84628-616-2/1).

## Verdict: adapt

Adapt the typed intersection service, explicit local edit contracts and failure-driven test cases; independently derive the numerical routines. Do not port the old pointer graph, ring-wise joining, generic relaxation marching or printed formulas unquestioningly. The public appendices are sufficient for useful prototypes, but this is not a complete robust curved-Boolean implementation, and the inaccessible main chapters remain an explicit reading gap.
