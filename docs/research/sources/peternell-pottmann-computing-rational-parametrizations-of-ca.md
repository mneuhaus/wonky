## What it is

DOCUMENTED: constructive proof that a **real** canal surface generated by rational sphere centers m(t) and rational radii r(t) admits a real rational parametrization. Constant-radius pipe surfaces are a special case. Theorem 5.1 is a **sufficient condition**, explicitly not a characterization of all rational canal surfaces. It does not establish that an arbitrary rolling-ball fillet has a rational spine, or that every surface offset is rational. [Introduction, §5/Theorem 5.1 and its remark](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

The most immediately useful implementation is not the general existence proof: it is the simple **reflection construction when one rational contact curve is already known**, especially a canal tangent to a plane. The fully general construction introduces polynomial factorization, coordinate adaptation and degree reduction with acknowledged numerical problems. [§§3.2, 4, 5 and 6](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

## How it works

### Envelope and regularity foundation

DOCUMENTED, equations (2.1)–(2.4):

    |x−m(t)|² − r(t)² = 0
    (x−m(t))·m′(t) + r(t)r′(t) = 0
    x(t,u) = m(t) + r(t)q(t,u),  |q|²=1,  q·m′+r′=0.

The derivative equation defines a plane; intersecting it with the generating sphere gives the characteristic circle. A real circle requires D=|m′|²−(r′)²≥0. Equality collapses the circle to a point; equality on an interval degenerates the envelope into a curve plus tangent planes. The construction assumes such equality is at most isolated. When the reality condition only holds on [a,b], the paper proposes t=(a+b*s²)/(1+s²), mapping the real s-axis into that interval. This is not a global nonsingularity guarantee. [§2, pp. 256–257](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

INFERRED directly from the two envelope equations: for |m′|>0 the circle center is m−r*r′*m′/|m′|² and its radius is r*sqrt(D)/|m′|. Thus a variable-radius canal is not the naïve circular sweep with radius r centered at m unless r′=0.

### Fast path: reflect one known rational envelope point

DOCUMENTED, §3.2/equation (3.2), p. 259:

1. Find a rational unit normal q̄(t) satisfying q̄·m′+r′=0, hence a known rational envelope point f=m+r*q̄.
2. For each t construct a pencil of planes through the spine tangent line. Plane normals are c(t,u)=v1(t)+u*v2(t), with v1,v2 independent and perpendicular to m′.
3. Reflect f in each of these planes:

       x(t,u) = f(t) − 2*r(t)*(c·q̄)/(c·c) * c.

This traces the characteristic circle rationally in u, including u=∞ in a second projective chart. Reflection preserves both the sphere and characteristic-plane conditions because the reflecting plane contains the tangent line through m. The paper offers v1=(m2′,−m1′,0), v2=(m3′,0,−m1′), but explicitly warns those vectors can be dependent. Its alternative is c=m′×n+u*n for an appropriate nonzero normal field n. [§3.2](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

INFERRED port rule: choose the reference coordinate axis least aligned with m′ to construct a nonzero rational perpendicular field, split chart intervals when that choice changes, and verify c·c is bounded away from zero over the patch. Do not normalize c: its scale cancels in the reflection quotient. Smooth patch seams need consistent parameter/chart transitions.

### Especially useful: tangent-plane seed, not general root solving

DOCUMENTED, §4/equation (4.1): if every generating sphere is tangent to z=0, choose r=m3 and f=(m1,m2,0). Then

    x(t,u) = (m1,m2,0) + 2*c3*m3/(c1²+c2²+c3²) * (c1,c2,c3).

This is rational directly whenever m is rational; no general sum-of-squares factorization is needed. Pipes with planar rational spines are a special case after positioning the tangent plane. The paper also constructs canals tangent to a sphere along a rational curve, and more generally canals from a rational contact curve f on a surface with rational unit normal n by m=f+r*n. [§4, pp. 259–260](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

INFERRED application: a known rational contact curve can make a plane-supported blend exportable even when its full rational parametrization would look complicated. But the contact curve/spine must actually be rational; an approximated intersection curve does not satisfy the exact hypothesis merely because its fitted spline is rational.

### General algorithm: stereographic Gauss map plus sum of two squares

DOCUMENTED, §5, equations (5.1)–(5.5):

1. Map unit normals q to a plane using stereographic projection from W=(0,0,1). The circle family on the unit sphere becomes planar circles with centers

       n(t)=−(m1′,m2′)/(r′+m3′),
       ρ²(t)=D/(r′+m3′)².

   If r′+m3′=0 the projected circle becomes a line; a coordinate/chart change is required.
2. Clear denominators. A globally nonnegative real polynomial F(t) can be written F=a²+b² (Lemma 5.1), proved by factoring over the complex numbers. Apply this to the numerator of ρ², obtaining a rational planar vector g with g·g=ρ².
3. Set a seed point φ̄=n+g on each planar circle. For d(u)=(u,1), reflect around diameters:

       φ(t,u)=φ̄(t)−2*(g·d)/(d·d)*d.

4. Apply inverse stereographic projection:

       q=(2φ1, 2φ2, φ1²+φ2²−1)/(1+φ1²+φ2²),
       x=m+r*q.

All operations after obtaining a,b are rational. **The coefficients of a,b need not be rational numbers**: “rational parametrization” refers to quotients of parameter polynomials over real coefficients. [§5](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

### Degree reduction is substantive, not free simplification

DOCUMENTED: §6 chooses a stereographic frame using a conjugate complex pair, or real double root, of D. A carefully chosen projection makes a known quadratic factor common to all four homogeneous normal coordinates, which can then be canceled. Equations (6.1)–(6.3) derive the adapted orthonormal frame; (6.4)–(6.5) give the homogeneous coefficients and cancellation conditions. Corollary 6.1 gives, for the polynomial degree-k setting, normal degree 2k−4 and surface bidegree (3k−4,2). Rational center/radius laws with a degree-k common denominator yield normal degree 4k−6 and surface bidegree (5k−6,2). Treat the linear/constant degeneracies separately rather than blindly applying expressions that become negative at k=1. [§6, pp. 262–264](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

For example, the documented polynomial cubic case yields a (5,2) canal parametrization after degree reduction. This is an algebraic degree statement, not a promise of good sampling distribution or a low-degree spine for a given fillet.

## Robustness and guarantees

DOCUMENTED: theorem in exact real arithmetic; not a certified F32/f64 algorithm. The authors explicitly say that computing roots numerically makes the sum-of-squares decomposition inexact and makes the proposed common factor an **inexact divisor**. They call for combining algebraic and numerical methods. [§6.2, p. 264](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

DOCUMENTED: parameter lines may bunch unevenly along characteristic circles, creating almost-singular rational parametrizations even for a well-defined pipe surface. Restricting the spine to rational-speed/Pythagorean-hodograph-type curves is suggested as a remedy. A mathematical rationality theorem does not deliver a well-conditioned chart. [p. 265](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

INFERRED validation requirements: bound D away from zero on regular patches; check sphere and derivative-envelope residuals; certify chart denominators and nonzero Jacobian; compare independent surface normal/contact constraints; verify patch seams and separate global self-intersection from local regularity. If a numerical factorization is used, propagate coefficient errors to a geometric deviation bound rather than labeling the resulting B-spline exact without qualification. [Envelope identities and admitted implementation error](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

## Parallelism and performance

No timings, hardware, language, precision format or repository found. Figures 4/5/7 show modeled canals/pipes, not a benchmark. [PDF](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).

INFERRED: evaluate rational m,r,q and reflect samples independently with balanced fork-join; fixed-degree patches have uniform GPU work. Polynomial coefficient products admit balanced reductions. General complex-root isolation, branch pairing, coordinate-frame selection, approximate common-factor cancellation and adaptive chart splitting have irregular work and should be separate native stages, not a single homogeneous GPU evaluator. The known-contact fast path avoids the hardest stage entirely.

## Known failures, limitations, war stories

- D<0: no real characteristic circle; D=0: collapsed characteristic, not an ordinary regular patch. [§2](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).
- Simple v1/v2 choices in the reflection method can be linearly dependent. [§3.2](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).
- Stereographic denominator r′+m3′ can vanish; chosen projection can be bad. [§5](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).
- Root approximations spoil exact sum-of-squares identities and common-factor divisions. [§6.2](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).
- Parametric near-singularities persist on regular-looking pipes. [p. 265](https://www.dmg.tuwien.ac.at/geom/ig/peternell/canalsurf.pdf).
- The theorem does not find the correct support-surface intersection spine, trim the selected fillet band, or produce globally valid solid topology. Those are INFERRED missing pipeline stages, not reported bugs. No issue tracker accompanies the paper.

## Relevance for wonky

INFERRED priority: first implement §3.2/§4 as a standalone Bend rational-canal evaluator for cases with a known rational contact seed; use it for exact mathematical surface representations and STEP rational B-spline export. It can extend analytic fillets beyond torus/cylinder when a genuinely rational spine/contact is available. It does **not** turn a generic algebraic/nonrational offset-surface intersection into a rational spine.

Bend arithmetic fit: the fast path needs only fixed-size vectors, derivatives, dot/cross products and rational quotients; excellent for immutable arrays and uniform GPU evaluation. Normalize world coordinates to keep F32x2 products away from exponent overflow/underflow. Its ~48-bit mantissa does not change F32's exponent range. U32 multi-limb coefficients can exactly maintain rational polynomials and sign tests for D after denominators are cleared, but arbitrary real root factors require an algebraic-number representation or certified numerical enclosures. No FFI is needed conceptually, but porting the general root/factorization stage is a major native Bend project rather than a few formulas.

Persist a canal as `(center law, radius law, seed contact, chart, regularity interval, support provenance)`; derive rational patches as export artifacts. That preserves the true construction even if NURBS degree reduction is numerically awkward. For FDM, mesh against a certified distance bound on regular charts; uneven rational parameter spacing means a uniform parameter grid is not a reliable geometric tolerance.

## Verdict: adapt

Adapt the known-contact reflection and tangent-plane construction first. Treat the general rationality theorem as a roadmap, not a guaranteed exact-in-F32x2 converter. Defer full symbolic/numeric factorization until a concrete fillet case needs it; explicitly reject nonrational/uncertified inputs rather than silently fitting a spline and claiming exactness.
