## What it is
A Parasolid-inspired B-rep kernel (the entity names are Parasolid's: Region/Shell/Face/Loop/Fin/Edge/Vertex). Its selling point is an error contract rather than feature breadth: every Boolean, blend and local edit returns PASS, DECLINE (`Err`, input unchanged) or, forbidden, WRONG. It covers analytic quadrics (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus) and NURBS, Booleans, constant and variable fillets, chamfers, vertex blends, shell, draft, offset, loft, sweep, STEP export and partial STEP import, plus a WASM ABI. Its first consumer is an app called "fieldforge" (README roadmap M7).

## How it works
Numeric model (DOCUMENTED, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 2, `crates/keel-math/src/*`):
- f64 everywhere for metric geometry. Session tolerances are linear `1e-8` and angular `1e-11` rad, copied from Parasolid (`keel-math/src/tolerance.rs`). Per-entity `Vertex::tolerance` and `Edge::tolerance` are always at least that floor.
- Claimed exact layer: Shewchuk predicates via the `robust` crate (`predicates.rs`). There is also a `OneRoot` type, (a + b*sqrt(c))/d over BigInt, compared by Devillers et al. sign batteries (`algebraic.rs`, `bigint.rs`). Outward-rounded intervals widen by one ULP per operation (`interval.rs`).
- DOCUMENTED from code, contradicting the README: `grep` finds no call to `orient_2d`, `orient_3d` or `OneRoot` outside `keel-math` itself (only its own module and a bench). The Boolean path (`boolean.rs`, 11.4k lines) contains about 393 literal epsilons (`1e-7` x154, `1e-9` x104, `1e-6` x56, `2e-2` x11, ...), although the architecture doc says `tolerance.rs` is "the only module permitted to define epsilons". The SSI branches on raw f64 (`cos <= COINCIDENCE_ANG` in `ssi.rs::plane_cylinder`). So "exact topology decisions" is aspirational. The real decisions are f64 plus tolerance bands, with post-hoc gates on the result.

Topology (DOCUMENTED, ARCHITECTURE section 4, `keel-topo/src/entity.rs`, `body.rs`):
- Radial-edge / PES non-manifold structure. `Edge.radial: Vec<FinKey>` holds all fins in angular order. A `Face` has both `front_region` and `back_region`. Regions are first-class, so cellular (non-regularized) results keep internal walls.
- Arena keys serve as addresses. `EntityId(u64)` is a monotonic, never-reused stable name. The `BTreeMap<EntityId, AnyKey>` fixes the deterministic iteration order for the whole kernel.
- Every public mutation emits exactly one `OpReport` with a `Derivation` of Created, Modified, Generated, SplitChild or MergeResult (the OCCT Modified/Generated/Deleted contract, made native).
- Euler operators (`mvfs`, `mev`, `mef`, `mekr`, `mfkrh` and their inverses) are the only mutation path. `validate()` (fin rings, radial cycles, d-of-d, Euler-Poincare) runs after every public op in debug builds.

Boolean pipeline (DOCUMENTED, ARCHITECTURE section 6, `boolean.rs::assemble_boolean`):
1. Front-door checks. Decline when signed mesh volume < -1e-9 (inside-out operand). An AABB broad phase with a margin covers the tessellation under-reporting curved extents. Disjoint union is a verbatim merge. `preimprint_coincident_overlaps` cuts coplanar overlaps into both operands first. With no seams, one generalized winding number (GWN) probe per side at a guaranteed-interior point decides disjoint vs nested.
2. `seam_curves` runs SSI per face pair. It returns a `SeamCurve {curve, closed, tol, on_boundary_a/b}`. Tangential or unassemblable seams hard-decline.
3. `imprint_operand` clones each operand and splits the seams in (`imprint.rs`, per-face planar overlay).
4. `classify_faces` (boolean.rs:2146). It picks one interior sample per fragment and computes a GWN against the other operand's triangle soup (`winding.rs::gwn_over`, Van Oosterom-Strackee `2*atan2(num, den)` per triangle, in f64). Geometric coincidence (`coincident_sense_at`) is tested first. `|w-0.5| < 0.25` is the on-boundary band, resolved by a two-sided probe at `eps = max(100*tol, 1e-6)` along the normal (`wp,wm < 0.25` means out, `> 0.75` means in, otherwise `OnOther(Unknown)`, which leads to a decline).
5. `select_faces` applies the Requicha/Laidlaw r-set tables. Coincident overlaps keep operand A's copy.
6. `stitch_by_import` is an identity-preserving glue that pairs seam coedges 1:1. The older polygon-soup stitcher was retired (Add. 165); a stitch failure now declines.
7. Post-condition gates (section 10), all of which convert to DECLINE. Positive finite volume. Analytic mass (divergence theorem per face, Gauss-Legendre with a "Green-slab" boundary fold for trimmed curved faces) must equal mesh volume: about 1e-9 when all faces are planar, a 2% chordal band when curved. The result volume must lie in an interval derived from the operand volumes (a union lies in `[max(va,vb), va+vb]`). The per-component mesh volume must stay within 25% of the mass. For cylinder/cone x sphere, an exact 1-D-integral op-volume serves as truth; for cylinder x cylinder, a voxel integral (CAPABILITIES 4.4).
- Tolerant tier (Add. 180): `boolean_tolerant(a,b,op,tol,fuzz) -> (BoolResult, Confidence{salvaged, tier, achieved_tolerance})`. It snaps near-parallel planar faces within `(0, fuzz]` exactly onto the partner plane, with fuzz capped at 1/10 of the shortest edge, then runs the strict pipeline once. The contract ("epsilon-solidity", Qi-Shapiro style): the volume deviation is at most `achieved_tolerance * total surface area`. The strict `boolean()` never snaps.

SSI (DOCUMENTED, `keel-geom/src/ssi.rs`):
- Result classes are Empty, Points, Curves and Coincident (Patrikalakis-Maekawa).
- Analytic pairs are closed-form. Plane/plane gives a line. Plane/cylinder gives lines, a circle or an exact `Ellipse3` (semi-major r/cos, semi-minor r). Plane/cone gives a circle or an ellipse via the Dandelin construction; parabola and hyperbola decline.
- Cylinder/cylinder has three rungs. Parallel axes give 0-2 ruling lines. Equal radii with intersecting axes give two exact ellipses in the planes with normals az-bz and az+bz. The general case uses `quadratic_branch_field` (ssi.rs:1079): parameterize the ruled surface A by (theta, v). The other quadric gives `q2 v^2 + q1(theta) v + q0(theta) = 0`, with `q2 = |P_b(az)|^2`, `q1 = 2 w.P_b(az)`, `q0 = |w|^2 - rb^2`, `w = P_b(c(theta) - ob)`, and `P_b` the projection off B's axis. The discriminant is sampled at N=1024 thetas: all positive means two wrap curves; each positive run is a "bite" loop. Bite ends are bisected (80 iterations). A Chebyshev substitution `theta = mid + half*cos(phi)` regularizes the sqrt turnaround. Each branch is least-squares fitted with a cubic NURBS (16..256 control points), accepted when the maximum deviation against fresh samples, times SAFETY=2, is at most tol; otherwise it declines. The same field serves cylinder/sphere and cone/sphere.
- NURBS x analytic works by implicit composition into a bivariate Bernstein polynomial followed by 2-D tracing. NURBS x NURBS uses Gauss-map normal-cone separability, subdivision seeds, and Krawczyk-verified marching. After "canonical recovery" (`recover.rs`), NURBS that are secretly quadrics are converted back to analytic form (Add. 34: NURBS x NURBS 190 s to 0.68 s).

Fillet (DOCUMENTED, ARCHITECTURE section 7, `blend.rs`, 7.8k lines): the rolling-ball construction, `spine = SSI(offset(Sa, r), offset(Sb, r))`. Plane/plane gives an exact `Cylinder3`. A plane/cylinder rim with the plane perpendicular to the axis gives an exact `Torus3` (convex needs `r_cyl > 2r`, concave uses `major = r_cyl + r`); a non-perpendicular plane/cylinder is a cyclide and declines. Convexity is decided by a GWN probe along the in-face bisector. Vertex blends: sphere octant, Varady-Rockwood setback, Charrot-Gregory patches (Add. 150, 176).

Tessellation (`tessellate.rs`): edge-first watertight. Faces fan to the shared edge samples, with `n >= span*sqrt(r/(8 tol))` chords per arc. A war-story fix: ear clipping that cannot finish completes the rest as a signed fan, which preserves signed area exactly by the shoelace formula (Add. 188).

## Robustness and guarantees
- What is actually guaranteed (INFERRED from code): nothing is proven. A body is PASS when it validates structurally and its volume equals (a) the analytic mass and (b) the operand-volume bounds within the tolerances above. Volume agreement is necessary, not sufficient. A wrong body with the right volume (a mirrored feature, a misplaced hole) passes every gate.
- Oracle harness mechanics (DOCUMENTED, `crates/keel-topo/tests/three_bucket.rs`, 442 lines), concrete enough to re-implement:
  - RNG: a 64-bit LCG `s = s*6364136223846793005 + 1442695040888963407` (Knuth MMIX constants), `f = (s >> 11) / 2^53`. Separate seeds per lane (`0x9E3779B97F4A7C15` box, `0xC0DE5EC701234567` cone), so adding a lane never shifts another lane's buckets.
  - Seeking: `KEEL_ORACLE_START` skips `start*12` draws (12 draws per box trial), which is linear, not O(1). Shards run `START = i*chunk, N = chunk` and their bucket counts sum exactly to the single-process run.
  - Box trial: origins `-5 + 10u`, extents `0.5 + 5u` per axis; `trial % 3` selects Union, Intersection or Difference; the reference is the exact interval-overlap formula (`va + vb - overlap`, `overlap`, `va - overlap`).
  - Contact lane: every fourth trial (`trial % 4 == 3`) places B against A's +face on axis `trial % 3` at offset `delta` cycling through `{0, +1e-9, -1e-9, +1e-7, -1e-7}`, with the cross axes forced to overlap. The code comment gives the reason: "Random floats never touch, so without this lane the tolerant tier's whole class would be unmeasured."
  - Judge: a result carrying any fault other than the informational `Coincident` is a DECLINE. PASS needs `|mass - ref| <= 1e-9*(1+ref) + slack` and `|mesh - ref| <= 1e-7*(1+ref) + slack`, where slack is the tolerant tier's `achieved_tolerance * area`. An empty result is right only if `ref <= 1e-9 + slack`. Everything else is WRONG and prints its full trial context. A per-(lane, op, delta, outcome) breakdown map is printed at the end.
- The 1M-trial oracle (`tests/three_bucket.rs`) is narrow. The strict lane is random axis-aligned box pairs, with every fourth trial a contact case (delta in {0, ±1e-9, ±1e-7}), checked against exact interval volume. The cone lane is an axis-aligned frustum through a box top, checked against the closed-form frustum volume. Reported results (Add. 193): strict 955,946 PASS / 44,054 DECLINE / 0 WRONG; tolerant 250,000 / 0 / 0; cone 998,375 / 1,625 / 0. So 4.4% of axis-aligned box pairs decline.
- The realistic soak ("8,039 of 10,000 pass") defines curved WRONG as a mass/mesh gap above 25% (`realsoak.rs::audit`). The union-WRONG fix (`docs/research/kernel/union-mass-mesh-WRONG-FINDINGS.md`) added a kernel gate that mirrors the oracle's threshold "to DECLINE exactly the bodies the realsoak oracle calls WRONG". Gate and oracle are then the same predicate, so WRONG=0 on that lane is partly true by construction (INFERRED).
- The "10,000-workflow census WRONG 0" cites "LOG Add 315", but `docs/ENGINEERING-LOG.md` ends at Addendum 198. The evidence for the headline census is not in the tree (DOCUMENTED absence).
- WRONGs that were really found are instructive. There were 4 in 100k (Add. 187, ear-clip dropping area on a concave sliver face). The first tolerant WRONG was a judge bug (Add. 193). A multi-component union passed the curved gate because the gate used a single-reference tetra sum while the user-facing mesh volume summed per component (union-WRONG findings). Lesson: every cheap oracle has holes, and faithful workflows find what random primitives miss (CAPABILITIES section 2).

## Parallelism and performance (self-reported, single-threaded, from ENGINEERING-LOG)
- Box oracle 90 ms/trial down to 0.9 ms/trial. Drill difference 270 to 4.3 ms. Sphere difference 135 to 6.3 ms. Pin union 4.2 ms (Add. 189-192). Every speedup came from replacing a sampled NURBS fit with an exact closed form (convex-face centroid, affine planar pcurves, rational-quadratic circle pcurves, coaxial (theta, v) lines). Before that, a generic 64-sample cubic pcurve fit "escalating" against C0 corners or constant curvature at 1e-7 cost about 69-173 ms per closed seam.
- There is no parallelism in the kernel. The oracle is sharded across processes through a seekable LCG (`KEEL_ORACLE_START`).

## Known failures, limitations, war stories
- Filleting all 12 box edges declines at the first 3-edge corner. G2 fillets are absent. Compound-operand Booleans (a tool crossing an already featured body and its holes) are the dominant real decline class. Tilted cylinder/cylinder declines on render-mesh watertightness. General NURBS Booleans are uncertified (CAPABILITIES section 4).
- About 70% of fuzzer declines were artifacts of ill-posed random input. A provenance classifier (`KEEL_FAITHFUL=1`) separates the real gaps (CAPABILITIES section 5).
- Two recursions (union-find, assembly-DAG walk) overflowed the stack on deep op chains and were made iterative (CAPABILITIES section 6).
- The classification band was bistable because detection used 1e-7 while classification used 1e-6. Fix: derive both from the op tolerance as tol/2 (Add. 189). Directly relevant to wonky's explicit-tolerance rule.

## Cross-source comparison (see sibling notes)
- `remus-esaueng-remus.md` uses the same `(theta, v)`-quadratic construction for general cylinder/cylinder SSI and the same closed-form two-ellipse Steinmetz case. INFERRED: this is the common LLM recipe, not keel-specific insight.
- Plane/cylinder rim fillet bound: keel requires `r_cyl > 2r` for the inward convex torus (`blend.rs:261-263`). remus argues that the quarter-tube band used never reaches the spindle torus's self-intersecting lobe (`R + r cos v < 0` needs `|v| > arccos(-R/r) >= pi/2`), so `r < r_c` suffices (`remus crates/blend/src/analytic.rs:816-868`). Wonky should take remus's bound after its own check. Keel's refuses valid geometry, which is safe but over-conservative.
- vcad's PR #789 (`building-a-cad-kernel-in-one-night-cam-pedersen.md`) found that closedness thresholds cannot separate good from garbage, and switched to a probe-grid set-semantics oracle. That complements keel's volume-only WRONG definition; wonky should use both.

## Relevance for wonky
- The contract and the instrument are the valuable parts. A PASS/DECLINE/WRONG oracle with a seekable deterministic RNG, sharding, exact closed-form references, per-lane counts, a contact lane with sub-tolerance deltas, and decline-provenance classification maps onto wonky's "unsupported must fail explicitly" rule. Wonky can hold its bake-off prototypes to exactly this metric.
- Bend port of the harness (INFERRED): replace the sequential LCG with a counter-based U32 generator (for example a PCG or xorshift-multiply hash of `(lane_seed, trial, draw)`), so any trial is O(1)-addressable and a whole lane is a balanced fork-join over trial indices with per-bucket counts summed at the joins. Keep keel's delta schedule but express the contact offsets as multiples of wonky's declared linear tolerance (for example `{0, ±tol/10, ±10*tol}`) instead of absolute 1e-9/1e-7. F32x2 resolves about 2^-44 relative (about 6e-12 at 100 mm coordinates), so keel's absolute deltas are representable at FDM scale, but tying them to the declared tolerance keeps the lane meaningful if the tolerance or the model scale changes. Add a lane with deltas of a few F32x2 ulps to hit the exact-predicate path directly.
- Strengthen the oracle beyond keel. Volume alone is too weak. Wonky should add exact point-membership sampling against the CSG input (using wonky's own exact predicates on multi-limb U32), a face-set/area comparison, and the Hausdorff distance between the result mesh and a reference. Never let the kernel gate equal the oracle predicate.
- The `quadratic_branch_field` idea ports well to Bend for the blocked plane/cylinder-like and cylinder/cylinder SSI. It is uniform per-theta work (N=1024 discriminant samples is a perfect GPU call tree), and the math is only sqrt plus dot products. It is fine in F32x2 if the "certificate" is replaced by a real interval or Taylor bound. Keel's certificate is sample-based and can miss small bites between samples (INFERRED).
- The tolerant tier (snap within fuzz, report `achieved_tolerance` and `salvaged`, never snap silently, cap fuzz at 1/10 of the shortest edge) matches wonky's explicit-tolerance rule. Adopt the report shape.
- The radial-edge structure with regions and `EntityId`-ordered determinism is mutation-heavy (arenas, Euler ops) and a poor fit for Bend's affine arrays. Wonky's existing immutable identity/provenance model is closer to what Bend needs. Copy the idea of `Derivation {Created, Modified, Generated, SplitChild, MergeResult}`, not the storage.
- Rolling-ball fillet specialisations (plane/plane to cylinder; perpendicular plane/cylinder to torus with its convexity and radius conditions; cyclide declines) are a good first fillet ladder for wonky's analytic surface set.
