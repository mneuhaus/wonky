## What it is
DOCUMENTED **from the primary abstract only**: an algorithm-design approach that gives combinatorial/topological consistency priority over numerical calculations. The basic algorithm is expressed as combinatorial and topological computation and may have several permissible next branches. Numerical computation chooses the branch that appears most likely to produce the correct geometric answer. The abstract states that this yields robust, simple algorithms and says the paper illustrates the method with examples. It does not expose those examples or their detailed theorems in the accessible preview. [Abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s004530010002).

## How it works
DOCUMENTED: the abstract's separation is **legal discrete transitions first, numerical branch preference second**. This differs from an ordinary geometric algorithm that allows independently rounded predicates to drive arbitrary topology edits. [Abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s004530010002).

INFERRED abstraction, **not extracted pseudocode**: define a state invariant I and a set of allowed transitions A(s). Require I(s) ⇒ I(a(s)) for every a∈A(s); then approximate scores can rank the allowed transitions without invalidating I. Termination, existence of an allowed action, and geometric accuracy are separate obligations. The full paper is needed to learn precisely how its examples establish these obligations and what their data structures are. The accessible evidence does not justify inventing its transition rules, numeric types or complexity bounds.

## Robustness and guarantees
- DOCUMENTED: the abstract claims robustness because the basic combinatorial/topological computation is not contaminated by numerical errors. This is the authors' stated design guarantee, not an independently verified theorem from the inaccessible body. [Abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s004530010002).
- DOCUMENTED **supporting context from a different paper, read in full:** Yap's earlier discussion of Sugihara–Iri explicitly distinguishes problem-dependent combinatorial robustness from stability (an answer correct for a small perturbation of the input), and says stability is not automatically obtained. This is not evidence for the exact 2000 paper's individual examples. [Yap §4.2, printed p.14](https://cs.nyu.edu/~exact/doc/towards.pdf).
- INFERRED: do not summarize this as “any geometry algorithm never crashes at any precision.” The invariant must be established for the particular algorithm; integer index overflow, implementation bugs, memory exhaustion, NaN handling and invalid preconditions remain possible. Nor does an abstractly valid manifold necessarily have a valid non-self-intersecting geometric embedding.
- No quantitative geometric-error bound, tolerance policy or backward-error theorem could be verified from the accessible material. “Topologically valid” cannot be promoted to “the requested Boolean within tolerance” without such a certificate.

## Relevance for wonky
**INFERRED recommendations from the documented principle and supplied constraints:**
- Learn the topology-first separation for Boolean/fillet/offset assembly: generate only legal immutable connectivity candidates, then use certified geometry to accept one. A manifold/Euler check is necessary in some representations but not sufficient for correct solid classification, embedding, inside/outside orientation or clearance.
- Combine, rather than replace, the exact-predicate path: use the topology invariant to constrain construction and diagnose contradictions; retain exact/filtered decisions on supported cases. Do not turn an unresolved sign into an arbitrary “consistent enough” branch.
- A proposed approximate fallback needs an explicit contract: original-source mapping, measured/certified geometric deviation in a stated norm, operation semantics, collapsed-feature report, minimum-clearance checks and an opt-in approximation status. If certification fails or cannot be computed, return a structured unsupported/uncertain result. FDM tolerances do not excuse unreported vanished holes or merged components.
- Bend can encode immutable states and U32 topology IDs; no FFI is required for the idea. Fixed-size local candidate sets are more promising than global mutable graph repair, but no such locality bound is established by this source review. F32x2 and multi-limb predicates remain needed wherever geometry must actually be certified.
