## What it is
DOCUMENTED: Compile *whole known polynomial predicates* ahead of time, using bounds on input bit lengths to generate (1) a cheap floating-point sign filter and (2) a statically sized exact fallback. Avoid paying for generic operator overloading, dynamic expression recording, arbitrary-width loops, and allocation in every arithmetic operation. It also generates exact constructors and associates numeric size bounds with the resulting types. [§§3–5](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=6).

## How it works
- **Arithmetic language.** DOCUMENTED: integer constants/variables, +, -, *, assignments, conditionals, sign and fixed-arity tuples/macros. Inputs must have declared maximum bit lengths. A representation such as homogeneous point (x,y,z,w) represents rational Cartesian coordinates (x/w,y/w,z/w) without division in the predicates. [§2.1,§5](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=5).
- **Static size analysis.** DOCUMENTED: B(a±b)=1+max(B(a),B(b)); B(a*b)=B(a)+B(b). Apply recursively to every intermediate and output. Polynomial degree is a rough predictor: required bits scale approximately as input bits times total degree, but the recurrence supplies concrete bounds including addition growth. Constructors compose and can increase degree multiplicatively. [§4](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=8).
- **Static filter.** DOCUMENTED: IEEE double throughout, effective precision 53 bits; intermediates that fit exactly have zero arithmetic error. A forward maxerr bound propagates operand errors, operand magnitude bounds and a rounding term depending on the intermediate's bit length. The bound is compile-time data: at runtime accept sign only outside ±maxerr. Conservative bounds can filter badly when actual operands are much smaller than their declared maximum. Do not transplant its binary64 constants into F32. [§§2.2,4.1](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=9).
- **Critical correction to a common summary:** DOCUMENTED: LN's exact backend here is **not U32 multi-limb arithmetic**. It stores an integer as an unevaluated sum a0+...+am with ai=a'i*2^(r*i), ai represented by binary64. Its static analysis ensures arithmetic on the components is exact. The representation may be temporarily unnormalized; addition combines equal positions, and multiplication is the O(m²) convolution c_k=sum_j a_j*b_(k-j). Normalize only when needed to keep every component operation within the 53-bit exact significand capacity. [§4.2, equations 1–3](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=10).
- **Normalization.** DOCUMENTED: move high bits from ai into a_(i+1), leaving ai mod 2^(r*i+r), preserving the exact sum. LN used r=23 as the compromise between fewer components and room for accumulated products; an implementation trick uses a large alignment constant plus conversion to single precision rather than a slow modulus function. The weighted-component representation itself is limited by binary64's exponent range (paper gives integers up to 2^1023). [§4.2](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=10).
- **Generation and caching.** DOCUMENTED: Fig. 4 is a homogeneous orient3d input; Fig. 5 shows filter then generated exact straight-line fallback with temporary slots. Fig. 6 generates a plane through 3 homogeneous points; 31-bit inputs produce plane coefficients about 97 bits long. LN associates those bounds with a generated class. Constructors are **always exact** in this implementation; lazy construction is discussed as a possible extension, not a feature already present. [§5,Figs.4–7,§7.2](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=12).
- **Representation matters more than heroic arithmetic.** DOCUMENTED: segment endpoints affine plus intersections homogeneous yield maximum predicate degree 5; all-homogeneous endpoints would make it 8. Their polyhedral modeller keeps plane equations as primary data; Booleans introduce no new planes, and intersection vertices are triples of existing planes. Cached vertex–plane dot products replace many higher-cost orientation tests. [§§6.3,7.1](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=17).

## Robustness and guarantees
DOCUMENTED: within the declared integer-input range and IEEE arithmetic assumptions, the fallback evaluates the polynomial exactly, including zero. Filter rejection is uncertainty, not evidence of degeneracy. The paper does not claim to solve arbitrary algebraic/non-polynomial predicates. The application still needs correct degenerate-case logic. [§§2,4,7.2](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=8).

DOCUMENTED: Boolean operations in the plane-based example are mathematically exact for their current inputs, **but transformations round plane coefficients back to a bounded size**, then require reconstruction of consistent combinatorics. Transform followed by inverse need not restore the original solid. This approximation is separate from the exact predicate guarantee and must not be silently imported into wonky. [§§6.3,7.1](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=17).

## Parallelism and performance
- DOCUMENTED: no GPU or parallel implementation is presented. Static known-width arithmetic is the contribution, not measured multicore speedup. [Paper](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf).
- DOCUMENTED measurements: Fig. 3 counts 7 naive versus approximately 35 exact floating operations for affine orient2d at 31-bit inputs; affine orient3d 23 versus 110; homogeneous orient3d 45 versus 645. These are operation counts, not wall-clock ratios. [Fig.3](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=11).
- DOCUMENTED: one million homogeneous orient3d evaluations on an SGI 200 MHz effective-rate system took 1.6 s with naive double, 33 s with LN **exact** 31-bit operands; dynamic error tracking 19 s and expression-state recording 53 s were measured separately. This does not mean the filtered whole algorithm is 20× slower. [Appendix A,Fig.14](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=24).
- DOCUMENTED: in the approximately 250-face polyhedron/self-rotated-copy experiment, moving from large angles toward 10^-9 radians raised total runtime about 50%; exact vertex construction was approximately 7% of runtime. Segment experiments had 250 segments and about 5% exact coordinate comparisons for random input. Authors explicitly say they did not implement every full algorithm twice as separate float/exact baselines; their overall overhead estimates use component costs. [§6](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=14).

## Known failures, limitations, war stories
DOCUMENTED: fixed size is also an API burden: input and constructed points can have different classes despite identical geometric meaning, leading to dispatch proliferation. Many nested constructors make this unattractive. Static filter bounds lose tightness when coordinate scale changes. Arbitrarily many constructions cannot fit an a-priori bounded representation without a depth/input-domain contract. Choosing primary plane data versus derived vertex data changes both algebraic degree and storage cost. There is no issue tracker attached to the paper; these limitations are the authors' own §7 discussion. [§7](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=18).

## Relevance for wonky
INFERRED implementation plan grounded in §§4–7:
1. Define an immutable expression DAG with add/sub/mul, exact constants, finite input domain, construction kind/depth, and explicit input-exponent bounds. Compute per-node B and a conservative filter certificate at generation time.
2. Emit a specialized F32 filter (optional F32x2 second tier), then a **different exact backend**: fixed-length U32 limbs, not LN's weighted binary64 components. Emit limb-count-specialized pure functions and no dynamic numeric allocation. Use U32 carry/borrow and 16-bit partial products because Bend lacks uint64; size every convolution accumulator before choosing reduction order.
3. For F32 input as exact dyadics, the mantissa being 24 bits does **not** mean each integer is 24 bits after common scaling: exponent span must be included. F32x2 also needs both components decoded and the full exponent span budgeted. Reject NaN/Inf/out-of-domain before the filter.
4. Encode “certified positive/negative/zero” separately from “filter uncertain” and “unsupported width.” Reassociation, FMA contraction, subnormal handling, and new balanced reductions each require a bound matching the actual evaluation graph.
5. Run independent predicates via balanced fork–join; batch unresolved predicates by formula/width so GPU work is uniform. Small carries may remain sequential within a limb tuple. This is a natural Bend adaptation, not a parallel guarantee of the paper.
6. Use this first for orient2d/orient3d, rational interval ordering and homogeneous plane intersections in Boolean/arrangement topology. Preserve source planes through operations to avoid repeatedly increasing algebraic degree. Nonlinear SSI/fillet root decisions need a separate algebraic layer.

INFERRED testing plan: exact-zero and one-unit-off-zero determinants; maximum declared magnitudes; all carry/borrow boundaries; cancellation; translation across large exponent spans; input/constructed-point combinations. Compare generated code to an independent integer oracle later, not during the active CPU benchmark. Retain the rejected-filter rate as telemetry rather than weakening the error bound to improve it. [Motivation and construction tradeoffs](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/alt/vorlesungen/sem0304/p223-fortune.pdf#page=18).
