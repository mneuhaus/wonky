wonky

A Rust-based CAD kernel speaking FeatureScript & build123d. Exact, introspective, refuses instead of guessing. Checked against Onshape.

What

wonky runs unmodified Onshape FeatureScript (and build123d-style Python) locally and turns it into real boundary-representation solids: STEP and STL out, with measurements, sections and diffs in a built-in viewer. It is a language runtime and a geometry kernel in one, built so that every answer is either provably right or an explicit, named refusal.

Why

  • Parametric code is locked in. FeatureScript only runs inside Onshape's cloud; desktop CAD offers scripting APIs, not a language that lives in the document.
  • Kernels fail silently. Tolerance-based kernels merge tiny gaps, invent sliver faces or report a fillet as successful when it is not. You find out at the printer.
  • No CAD in CI. Parametric models cannot be built, diffed and tested offline like software.
  • Agents need a kernel they can trust. AI-assisted CAD needs deterministic results, certificates and introspection, not "looks about right".

How

  • Plain f64 geometry, exact decisions. Every sign and topology decision goes through exact predicates (expansion arithmetic, exact rationals behind fast filters). If a decision cannot be proven, wonky refuses by name. Never silent-wrong.
  • Decided from the construction. Tangency, incidence and gaps come from how the model was built, with construction provenance carried through transforms, not from a tolerance band.
  • Direct-better algorithms. Each operation is written as the best known exact algorithm, not as a port of an older kernel.
  • Validated from outside. Results are checked against Onshape (Parasolid) references, OCCT, closed-form solutions, metamorphic variants (moved, rotated, scaled models must agree) and independently replayed exact certificates.
  • Architecture. A FeatureScript interpreter and host drive a native Rust kernel: exact numerics, exact univariate algebra, analytic curves and surfaces, a B-rep with provenance, a versioned wire contract between them. Bend, the current production kernel, stays as a cross-check until Rust takes over.

An independent audit of every non-passing reference cell found one real kernel defect (OCCT turns a valid lens into an invalid solid); the other cells are documented tolerance behaviour or errors on our side (catalog, twin or measurement), and six disputed zones score zero for every kernel. Where we are (September 2026)

  • Project started on 22 September 2026. The FeatureScript interpreter, the viewer and the Bend kernel build 7 of 8 R20 modules (31 of 33 parts); the eighth is refused by name.
  • On 26 September the kernel moved to Rust: a plan of 75 packages up to "R20 with blends on Rust". The foundation (exact numerics, wire contract, native addon) and nine building blocks (exact algebra, validated transcendentals, degree-2 and radical predicates, a B-rep core, a sketch solver, an OCCT oracle, the construction contract) have landed, each through two final gates. The next wave (certified geometry, B-rep booleans, exact algebraic points) is in verification.
  • Verification works as intended: nearly every package had a real silent-wrong class caught before landing (a doubly covered shell certified with twice its volume, a false zero from underflow, lineage leaking through a rollback).
  • Honest estimate to "R20 with blends on Rust": two to three weeks, to be recalibrated after the first ten landed Rust packages.

Where it goes

Native, local, fast parametric CAD with FeatureScript outside the cloud; a kernel that explains its answers; CAD-Acid as an open benchmark for any kernel; later perhaps a native UI and bridges into desktop CAD such as Fusion.
